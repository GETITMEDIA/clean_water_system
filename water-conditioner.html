Water Conditioner and More

What does a water conditioner do?

A water conditioner prevents limescale build-up in plumbing by changing the chemical make-up of the water, without actually softening it — it's often called a "salt-free water softener." It uses a template-assisted crystallization (TAC) media to catch minerals like magnesium and calcium; these build up until they break off in the output water, which stays hard but can no longer stick to pipes and appliances.

The key difference between a conditioner and a softener is that a conditioner uses no salt and produces no wastewater — instead of flushing brine to regenerate resin, it simply causes calcium and magnesium crystals to form on nucleation sites within the TAC media. Conditioners aren't quite as effective at preventing limescale as softeners, but they're a significant improvement over having no treatment system at all.
